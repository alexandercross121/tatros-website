# Tatros User Personas

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the principal user groups whose needs should inform the information architecture, content structure, interaction patterns, and conversion pathways of the Tatros website. It is a shared reference for UX designers, researchers, content strategists, developers, and business stakeholders.

These personas are role-based behavioral archetypes, not demographic profiles. They represent common organizational contexts, decision criteria, constraints, and information needs among prospective Tatros clients. They should be validated and refined through interviews, sales data, analytics, and usability research.

The framework supports three objectives:

- Help visitors identify relevant Tatros capabilities without requiring prior technical knowledge.
- Provide sufficient evidence and risk-reduction information for complex B2B and public-sector decisions.
- Support coherent progress from problem recognition through evaluation, internal alignment, and qualified contact.

## 2. Primary Personas

### 2.1 Startup Founder

**Profile:** An entrepreneurial decision maker building or scaling a technology-enabled product. This may be a non-technical founder seeking a complete delivery partner or a technical founder seeking specialist capacity and execution support. Resources are constrained, timelines are compressed, and product decisions carry significant commercial consequences.

**Goals**

- Validate an opportunity and bring a credible product to market quickly.
- Define an achievable minimum viable product and prioritize high-value capabilities.
- Establish a technical foundation that can scale beyond the first release.
- Demonstrate traction to customers, investors, or partners.
- Access product, engineering, cloud, data, and AI expertise without prematurely building a large team.

**Challenges**

- Translating an evolving business concept into a stable, testable scope.
- Balancing speed, quality, cost, scalability, and technical debt.
- Estimating delivery effort while requirements and market assumptions remain uncertain.
- Coordinating design, engineering, infrastructure, security, and launch activities.
- Evaluating technical partners without formal procurement resources.

**Pain Points**

- Vendors that accept requirements without challenging weak assumptions.
- Unclear estimates, hidden dependencies, and uncontrolled scope expansion.
- Long discovery cycles that do not produce actionable decisions.
- Prototypes that cannot support real users or future investment.
- Limited visibility into progress, risks, and intellectual-property ownership.

**Technology Maturity:** Variable, from low to high. The founder may understand the market and product deeply while relying on Tatros for architecture and delivery decisions. Technical founders will expect detailed engineering dialogue and integration with existing teams or codebases.

**Buying Motivations**

- Faster, more predictable time to market.
- Confidence that the investment will produce a usable, extensible product.
- Multidisciplinary expertise through one accountable partner.
- Flexible engagement aligned with funding stages and changing priorities.
- Evidence of speed supported by sound engineering practices.

**Preferred Communication Style:** Direct, concise, practical, and collaborative. Communication should distinguish immediate priorities from future possibilities, surface trade-offs early, and relate technical decisions to validation, runway, and growth.

**Decision Making Process:** The founder is usually the economic buyer and final decision maker. A co-founder, technical advisor, investor, or fractional CTO may influence selection. The process commonly includes capability review, an introductory discussion, scope clarification, proposal comparison, and reference validation. Cost, team confidence, speed, and partnership fit strongly influence the outcome.

**Success Metrics**

- Time from approved scope to validated release.
- Delivery against budget and milestone expectations.
- Activation, adoption, retention, or transaction performance.
- Reduction in critical product and technical uncertainty.
- Platform stability and capacity for growth.
- Investor, partner, or early-customer readiness.

**Website Expectations**

- Immediate clarity on product development, AI, cloud, web, and mobile capabilities.
- A practical explanation of how Tatros moves from concept to delivered outcome.
- Startup-relevant case evidence and technical credibility.
- Transparent engagement stages, deliverables, and next steps.
- A low-friction route to discuss feasibility, priorities, timing, and budget range.

### 2.2 Small Business Owner

**Profile:** An owner or senior leader responsible for growth and daily performance in an organization with limited internal technology capacity. Technology is primarily viewed as an enabler of revenue, efficiency, customer service, resilience, and competitiveness.

**Goals**

- Improve operational efficiency and reduce repetitive manual work.
- Modernize customer-facing digital experiences.
- Integrate disconnected tools, processes, and data.
- Improve reliability, security, and access to technical support.
- Make technology investments with clear, proportionate business value.

**Challenges**

- Identifying which technology investments to prioritize.
- Operating with limited technical staff, budget, and implementation capacity.
- Understanding dependencies among digital products, cloud services, security, and business systems.
- Minimizing disruption during change.
- Comparing providers with materially different services and pricing models.

**Pain Points**

- Technical explanations that obscure costs, responsibilities, or business impact.
- Unresponsive providers and fragmented accountability.
- Previous investments that were difficult to maintain or failed to deliver value.
- Outages, slow systems, manual workarounds, and inconsistent data.
- Fear of unnecessary complexity or supplier dependency.

**Technology Maturity:** Typically low to moderate. The organization may use several cloud products but lack a coherent roadmap, integration strategy, governance model, or dedicated technology leadership.

**Buying Motivations**

- A clear business case and understandable return on investment.
- One dependable partner that can advise, deliver, and provide ongoing support.
- Reduced operational burden and technology risk.
- Predictable cost, scope, ownership, and support arrangements.
- Recommendations scaled appropriately to the organization.

**Preferred Communication Style:** Plain, outcome-oriented, patient, and transparent. Communication should avoid unnecessary terminology and make responsibilities, costs, risks, and decisions explicit.

**Decision Making Process:** The owner often initiates and approves the purchase, with input from finance, operations, employees, or an external IT advisor. Evaluation may begin with referrals or search, followed by service comparison, consultation, proposal review, and references. Trust, responsiveness, affordability, business understanding, and support are central criteria.

**Success Metrics**

- Hours of manual work eliminated or redirected.
- Reduced cost, errors, downtime, or support incidents.
- Increased qualified leads, conversions, revenue, or customer satisfaction.
- Employee adoption and process compliance.
- Predictable maintenance and support performance.
- Time to recover the technology investment.

**Website Expectations**

- Clear organization around recognizable business problems and outcomes.
- Accessible service descriptions that do not assume technical expertise.
- Examples from organizations of comparable scale or complexity.
- Explanations of delivery, support, ownership, security, and maintenance.
- Straightforward contact options and clear expectations for the first conversation.

### 2.3 Enterprise Executive (CEO, CTO, or CIO)

**Profile:** A senior executive accountable for enterprise growth, technology strategy, transformation, resilience, or operating performance. The persona may be an economic buyer, executive sponsor, or technical authority. Decisions affect multiple business units, systems, regulations, and stakeholder groups.

**Goals**

- Align technology investment with strategy and measurable business outcomes.
- Modernize legacy systems and operating models without compromising continuity.
- Scale AI, data, cloud, software, and automation initiatives responsibly.
- Improve agility, customer experience, productivity, and decision quality.
- Reduce technology risk, complexity, and total cost of ownership.
- Establish a partner capable of supporting long-term transformation.

**Challenges**

- Coordinating transformation across portfolios, teams, vendors, and governance structures.
- Integrating new solutions with legacy systems and enterprise architecture.
- Building a defensible business case across functions and time horizons.
- Managing security, privacy, compliance, resilience, and organizational change.
- Moving from pilots to enterprise adoption and measurable value.

**Pain Points**

- Providers that overstate capabilities or rely on generic transformation narratives.
- Technical proposals disconnected from operating realities and strategic priorities.
- Poor executive visibility into program health, dependencies, risk, and value.
- Vendor lock-in, weak documentation, and insufficient knowledge transfer.
- Technology outputs that fail to achieve sustained business adoption.

**Technology Maturity:** Moderate to advanced, with variation across business units. The organization likely has formal architecture, security, procurement, data governance, and delivery functions. The executive expects Tatros to complement internal expertise and operate within established controls.

**Buying Motivations**

- Demonstrated ability to solve complex, cross-functional problems at scale.
- Strategic and technical depth across advisory, engineering, cloud, AI, data, and managed services.
- Strong governance, security, delivery assurance, and executive reporting.
- Relevant evidence, references, and measurable outcomes.
- A collaborative model that strengthens internal capability.

**Preferred Communication Style:** Evidence-led, concise, structured, and calibrated to the stakeholder. Executive communication should emphasize outcomes, economics, strategic risk, and accountability; technical communication should provide sufficient depth for specialist reviewers.

**Decision Making Process:** The process is multi-stage and committee-led. It may include executive sponsorship, business-case development, architecture and security review, vendor evaluation, procurement, legal review, financial approval, and board oversight. A proof of concept, discovery engagement, or phased program may precede broader commitment. Decisions depend on capability fit, risk posture, commercial terms, delivery model, references, and stakeholder confidence.

**Success Metrics**

- Revenue, cost, productivity, or margin impact attributable to the initiative.
- Adoption and benefit realization across business units.
- Reduced technical debt, operational risk, or total cost of ownership.
- Delivery predictability across milestones, budget, quality, and scope.
- System performance, availability, security, and compliance outcomes.
- Improved time to market and organizational delivery capacity.

**Website Expectations**

- A clear account of Tatros's strategic, technical, and delivery capabilities.
- Enterprise case studies with context, approach, scale, and quantified outcomes where permissible.
- Specific information about security, governance, quality, accessibility, integration, and support.
- Evidence of sector knowledge, senior expertise, delivery maturity, and long-term viability.
- Content suitable for business, technical, risk, and procurement stakeholders.
- A credible path to an executive briefing or structured discovery engagement.

### 2.4 Government Decision Maker

**Profile:** A public-sector executive, program leader, department head, or technology authority responsible for improving public services or procuring technology within formal accountability structures. This persona balances citizen outcomes, policy objectives, stewardship of public funds, security, accessibility, and procurement compliance.

**Goals**

- Improve the accessibility, reliability, and usability of public services.
- Modernize legacy systems and manual administrative processes.
- Strengthen data-informed decisions while protecting sensitive information.
- Deliver policy and program outcomes within approved budgets and timelines.
- Improve interoperability, resilience, security, and continuity.
- Select suppliers that meet public-sector delivery standards.

**Challenges**

- Navigating procurement, budget cycles, approvals, and contractual requirements.
- Serving populations with varying access needs, languages, devices, and digital literacy.
- Integrating with aging systems, shared infrastructure, and mandated standards.
- Managing privacy, records, cybersecurity, accessibility, and data-sovereignty obligations.
- Sustaining programs across leadership changes and policy shifts.

**Pain Points**

- Suppliers that underestimate governance, documentation, accessibility, or security.
- Proposals that are difficult to assess against formal requirements.
- Delays caused by poorly managed dependencies and stakeholder alignment.
- Proprietary solutions that limit portability, transparency, or procurement flexibility.
- Insufficient handover, training, support, and institutional knowledge transfer.

**Technology Maturity:** Variable and heterogeneous. Mature governance and security processes may coexist with legacy technology and uneven digital capability. The buyer expects compliance with applicable standards and disciplined collaboration with internal teams and incumbent suppliers.

**Buying Motivations**

- Demonstrable public value and responsible use of funds.
- Confidence in security, privacy, accessibility, resilience, and compliance.
- Capacity to deliver within formal governance and reporting structures.
- Transparent methods, documentation, pricing, and accountability.
- Sustainable solutions internal teams can operate and evolve.

**Preferred Communication Style:** Formal, precise, evidence-based, and non-promotional. Information should map clearly to requirements, risks, standards, deliverables, governance, and measurable public outcomes. Claims should be substantiated and terminology consistent.

**Decision Making Process:** The process is structured, documented, and frequently constrained by procurement rules. Stakeholders may include program leadership, IT, security, privacy, legal, finance, procurement, accessibility specialists, and executive oversight. Typical stages include market research, requirements definition, budget approval, solicitation, written evaluation, demonstrations, due diligence, award, and contract negotiation.

**Success Metrics**

- Service adoption, completion rates, processing time, and citizen satisfaction.
- Accessibility conformance and equitable service access.
- Reduced administrative workload, errors, cost, or backlog.
- Availability, resilience, security, and incident performance.
- Compliance with scope, budget, schedule, reporting, and regulations.
- Successful knowledge transfer, operational readiness, and maintainability.

**Website Expectations**

- Clear public-sector and digital-transformation capabilities.
- Verifiable evidence of relevant delivery experience and competence.
- Accessible, standards-aligned content that demonstrates delivery discipline.
- Information on security, privacy, governance, accessibility, quality, documentation, and support.
- Shareable capability information suitable for internal review and market research.
- Unambiguous organizational identity, contact details, and procurement inquiry routes.

## 3. Secondary Personas

Secondary personas commonly influence requirements, shortlist providers, evaluate risk, or manage implementation. Their needs should be supported without displacing primary audience pathways.

### 3.1 Marketing Director

- **Role:** Business sponsor or functional buyer for customer experience, acquisition, analytics, content platforms, and marketing technology.
- **Core needs:** Stronger customer journeys, conversion performance, brand consistency, content operability, reliable analytics, and system integration.
- **Evaluation criteria:** Relevant experience, user-centered methods, measurable outcomes, accessibility, integration capability, and speed of iteration.
- **Website expectations:** Digital experience case studies, clear methods, measurable outcomes, platform capabilities, and evidence connecting experience design with engineering and data.

### 3.2 Operations Manager

- **Role:** Problem owner, process expert, implementation lead, or operational approver for automation, internal applications, integration, analytics, and managed services.
- **Core needs:** Less process friction, fewer manual handoffs, improved visibility, reliable workflows, minimal disruption, and adoption support.
- **Evaluation criteria:** Understanding of current operations, integration ability, implementation risk, maintainability, support responsiveness, and efficiency gains.
- **Website expectations:** Operational transformation examples, delivery stages, integration information, post-launch support, and outcome-oriented descriptions.

### 3.3 IT Manager

- **Role:** Technical evaluator, delivery counterpart, security reviewer, service owner, or operational gatekeeper.
- **Core needs:** Architectural compatibility, secure integration, maintainable systems, documentation, observability, support, knowledge transfer, and clear ownership.
- **Evaluation criteria:** Engineering standards, architecture, cloud and security competence, development lifecycle, testing, deployment, service levels, and avoidance of unnecessary lock-in.
- **Website expectations:** Technical depth supporting business claims, including architecture, integration, security, governance, quality assurance, support models, and technical case evidence.

### 3.4 Procurement Officer

- **Role:** Sourcing process owner, commercial evaluator, compliance reviewer, and contracting stakeholder.
- **Core needs:** Comparable scope and pricing, supplier credentials, contractual clarity, compliance evidence, manageable risk, accurate documentation, and responsive communication.
- **Evaluation criteria:** Eligibility, organizational stability, required insurance or certifications, references, pricing, data handling, subcontractor transparency, terms, and performance accountability.
- **Website expectations:** Consistent corporate information, bounded capabilities, procurement contacts, relevant policies, shareable credentials, and verifiable claims.

## 4. User Journey Summary

The website should support a common decision journey while allowing each persona to enter through the problem, service, industry, or evidence most relevant to them. The journey is not necessarily linear; enterprise and government users may revisit evidence and assurance content as additional stakeholders join the evaluation.

| Persona | Entry and orientation | Relevance assessment | Evaluation and validation | Preferred conversion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Startup Founder | Enters through search, referral, capability content, or a product insight and confirms startup relevance. | Connects product strategy, software, AI, cloud, web, or mobile capabilities to the immediate need. | Reviews relevant work, delivery approach, team credibility, engagement structure, and evidence of speed and scalability. | Requests a focused feasibility or discovery conversation with minimal friction. |
| Small Business Owner | Enters through a business problem, referral, search, or service need and seeks clarity without technical interpretation. | Connects a recognizable operational or customer problem to an appropriately scaled solution. | Reviews comparable outcomes, process, ownership, support, cost drivers, and trust indicators. | Requests a consultation or assessment through a straightforward contact route. |
| Enterprise Executive | Enters through thought leadership, referral, industry content, a case study, or a transformation capability. | Assesses strategic alignment, capability depth, enterprise maturity, and capacity to operate at scale. | Shares case and assurance content with architecture, security, finance, legal, and procurement stakeholders. | Requests an executive briefing, capability discussion, or structured discovery engagement. |
| Government Decision Maker | Enters through market research, referral, public-sector content, or formal sourcing activity. | Confirms relevance to public outcomes and baseline competence in accessibility, security, and governance. | Reviews credentials, methods, policies, evidence, and procurement suitability with a multidisciplinary committee. | Uses a procurement-oriented inquiry route or requests capability information. |
| Marketing Director | Enters through experience, web, analytics, or transformation content. | Maps capabilities to acquisition, content, experience, and measurement needs. | Reviews outcomes, integrations, accessibility, methods, and analytics maturity. | Requests a cross-functional solution or discovery discussion. |
| Operations Manager | Enters through automation, integration, managed services, BI, or process content. | Identifies fit for operational bottlenecks and constraints. | Reviews disruption controls, integrations, adoption, support, and outcomes. | Requests an operational assessment or use-case discussion. |
| IT Manager | Enters through technical content or a link shared by another stakeholder. | Tests whether claims are supported by credible technical detail. | Reviews architecture, security, lifecycle, integration, support, and documentation. | Requests a technical workshop or joins a stakeholder discussion. |
| Procurement Officer | Enters through supplier research, shared content, or due diligence. | Confirms identity, service scope, eligibility, and required information. | Evaluates credentials, policies, references, terms, commercial structure, and risk. | Uses a designated procurement or corporate contact route. |

Following conversion, every persona should receive a clear response timeframe, preparation guidance, named next step, and an explanation of what Tatros requires to assess the opportunity responsibly.

## 5. UX Recommendations

### 5.1 Cross-Persona Considerations

- Structure content around user problems, desired outcomes, services, and relevant contexts rather than internal terminology alone.
- Provide entry routes by service, industry, organizational need, and transformation stage.
- Use progressive disclosure: lead with decision-relevant summaries and make technical, operational, and assurance details available on demand.
- Separate substantiated evidence from capability claims. Case studies should explain context, constraints, Tatros's role, approach, outputs, and outcomes where disclosure permits.
- Explain discovery, scoping, delivery, governance, handover, support, and responsibilities.
- Keep terminology and factual claims consistent across service pages, cases, policies, capability materials, and contact paths.
- Support internal sharing with stable URLs, descriptive titles, concise summaries, and accessible downloadable information where justified.
- Treat accessibility, performance, security, privacy, and responsive behavior as baseline experience requirements.
- Match contact pathways to visitor intent and evaluation stage; request only information needed to route and prepare the next interaction.
- Measure decision progress through qualified inquiries, evidence consumption, return visits, content sharing, journey completion, and key drop-off—not page views alone.

### 5.2 Startup Founder

- Prioritize rapid orientation, concise capability mapping, and a clear account of how discovery reduces uncertainty.
- Make trade-offs among speed, scope, quality, and scalability understandable without prescribing a premature solution.
- Connect product, AI, cloud, web, and mobile pathways rather than presenting isolated service silos.
- Provide evidence relevant to launch, validation, technical foundations, and scaling without implying guaranteed commercial outcomes.
- Keep inquiry lightweight while allowing the founder to provide stage, objective, timeline, existing assets, and constraints.

### 5.3 Small Business Owner

- Use plain-language labels and recognizable business scenarios before technical categories.
- Explain implementation implications, including disruption, staff involvement, migration, training, ownership, maintenance, and support.
- Clarify when custom delivery, integration, managed services, or advisory work may be appropriate.
- Reinforce trust through specific process information, organizational identity, accessible contacts, and realistic evidence.
- Do not require visitors to know their desired technical solution before contacting Tatros.

### 5.4 Enterprise Executive

- Establish a clear hierarchy from strategic outcomes to capabilities, methods, evidence, and assurance.
- Support executive scanning and specialist due diligence through layered content and purposeful cross-linking.
- Present cases with organizational context, complexity, governance, integration considerations, and measured impact.
- Make delivery maturity visible through governance, risk, security, quality, change enablement, and value measurement.
- Provide high-intent routes to executive and technical discussions rather than a generic sales path.

### 5.5 Government Decision Maker

- Meet applicable accessibility requirements throughout the experience and provide accessible alternatives for downloads.
- Use precise, verifiable language; avoid ambiguous superlatives and unsupported claims.
- Make public-sector capabilities, governance, security, privacy, documentation, accessibility, and support easy to locate.
- Design materials for committee review and requirements comparison, with clear ownership, versioning, and contacts.
- Support early market research and formal procurement boundaries without pressuring premature commitment.

### 5.6 Secondary Personas

- Give Marketing Directors direct access to customer-experience methods, analytics, content operations, integrations, accessibility, and outcomes.
- Give Operations Managers clear views of process discovery, automation, implementation risk, adoption, support, and operational measurement.
- Give IT Managers sufficient detail on architecture, engineering standards, security, cloud, integration, testing, deployment, observability, documentation, and knowledge transfer.
- Give Procurement Officers a dependable path to corporate credentials, compliance information, policies, references, commercial contacts, and maintained supplier documentation.
- Ensure secondary personas can move between specialist information and the primary sponsor's business case without losing context.

## Research and Validation Note

These personas are planning hypotheses, not validated representations. Tatros should test them through prospective and existing client interviews, internal stakeholder workshops, inquiry and proposal analysis, search behavior, analytics, and moderated usability studies. Research should examine differences by sector, organization size, geography, procurement model, technology maturity, and engagement type. Findings should refine persona priorities, language, journey assumptions, decision criteria, and success measures.
