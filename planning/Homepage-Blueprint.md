## Purpose

The homepage is the principal orientation and routing page for Tatros. Its objective is to help a broad range of visitors understand what Tatros does, determine whether its capabilities are relevant, verify organizational credibility, and select an appropriate next step.

The homepage must support visitors arriving with different levels of awareness:

- **Problem-aware:** The visitor recognizes a business or operational challenge but does not know which technology approach is appropriate.
- **Solution-aware:** The visitor is evaluating a defined capability such as artificial intelligence, software development, cloud solutions, or IT consulting.
- **Provider-aware:** The visitor is validating Tatros following a referral, shortlist, procurement activity, or prior interaction.
- **Talent-aware:** The visitor is evaluating Tatros as a prospective employer and needs a clear route to Careers without disrupting the client journey.

The page should provide enough information to establish relevance and trust, but it should not attempt to reproduce every service, industry, case study, or corporate detail. Its primary architectural role is to create informed onward movement.

### Scope

This blueprint defines content hierarchy, section responsibilities, expected user actions, conversion intent, responsive priorities, and implementation constraints. It does not define final copy, visual design, HTML, CSS, or component implementation.

### Homepage Success Indicators

- Meaningful progression from Home to Services, Industries, Case Studies, Portfolio, Resources, About, or Contact.
- Qualified contact initiations and completed inquiries attributable to homepage journeys.
- Engagement with evidence and decision-support content, not raw scroll depth alone.
- Reduced immediate exits among relevant visitors.
- Successful navigation by persona, device, input mode, and entry context.
- High task completion in first-click and usability testing.

## Primary User Goals

### Startup Founder

The Startup Founder wants to establish whether Tatros can turn an idea, early product, or scaling challenge into a credible and extensible solution. The founder prioritizes speed, practical guidance, multidisciplinary delivery, scope control, ownership clarity, and evidence that Tatros can balance near-term launch needs with long-term technical quality.

**Homepage questions:**

- Does Tatros work with startups and evolving product requirements?
- Can one team support product strategy, software, AI, cloud, web, or mobile delivery?
- Is there evidence of practical execution and scalable engineering?
- What is the first step, and how quickly can I determine fit?

**Preferred onward routes:** Services, Industries: Startups, Portfolio, Case Studies, and Contact.

### Business Owner

The Business Owner wants to understand how technology can improve operations, customer experience, revenue, reliability, or support without first translating the problem into technical terminology. The owner prioritizes understandable outcomes, an appropriately scaled approach, transparent responsibility, predictable support, and a trustworthy long-term partner.

**Homepage questions:**

- Does Tatros understand business problems rather than only technical tasks?
- Which services apply to my situation?
- Can Tatros advise, deliver, and support the solution?
- Is there evidence from comparable challenges or organizations?

**Preferred onward routes:** Services, Industries: Small Business, Our Process, FAQ, Case Studies, and Contact.

### Enterprise Executive

The Enterprise Executive wants to assess strategic alignment, delivery maturity, technical breadth, governance, and ability to operate across complex systems and stakeholder groups. This visitor may need to share homepage destinations with architecture, security, operations, finance, legal, and procurement reviewers.

**Homepage questions:**

- Can Tatros address complex, cross-functional transformation needs?
- Does the company have sufficient strategic and engineering depth?
- Are its claims supported by relevant evidence and measurable outcomes?
- Can Tatros operate within enterprise governance, security, and integration constraints?

**Preferred onward routes:** Industries: Enterprise, relevant Services, Case Studies, About, Resources, and Contact.

### Government Decision Maker

The Government Decision Maker wants to determine whether Tatros can support public-service and operational outcomes within formal accessibility, security, privacy, governance, and procurement requirements. Information must be precise, verifiable, and suitable for internal circulation.

**Homepage questions:**

- Does Tatros understand government delivery and public accountability?
- Are relevant capabilities, organizational details, and evidence easy to verify?
- Does the experience itself demonstrate accessibility and delivery discipline?
- Is there a clear, procurement-appropriate contact route?

**Preferred onward routes:** Industries: Government, relevant Services, Case Studies, About, FAQ, policies, and Contact.

## Homepage Objectives

### Establish Credibility

Communicate Tatros's identity, focus, and delivery posture immediately. Support claims with real evidence, specific capability information, a clear process, and verified organizational details.

### Communicate Services

Present the principal service portfolio in language that connects technical capability to recognizable business needs. Allow users to reach service details without scanning an exhaustive catalog.

### Generate Qualified Leads

Encourage appropriate visitors to initiate a conversation after receiving enough context to describe their need. Optimize for inquiry quality and mutual fit rather than maximum form volume.

### Showcase Expertise

Demonstrate strategic, technical, and sector understanding through case studies, portfolio work, responsible innovation content, delivery methods, and current insights.

### Increase Meaningful Conversions

Support both primary conversion and high-value micro-conversions, including service exploration, case-study review, industry selection, resource engagement, and return visits.

### Build Trust

Make the company, methods, responsibilities, evidence, policies, accessibility, and contact expectations understandable. Avoid unverified logos, generic metrics, unsupported superlatives, or artificial urgency.

### Enable Persona-Based Routing

Provide routes for startup, small-business, enterprise, and government visitors without requiring a separate homepage for each audience.

## Complete Homepage Structure

The following sequence is the recommended content hierarchy. Sections may be combined when research or content readiness demonstrates that consolidation improves comprehension. They must not be reordered solely for visual variety.

### 1. Global Header and Primary Navigation

**Purpose:** Establish identity and provide persistent access to the approved information architecture before the visitor engages with homepage content.

**Content:**

- Tatros identity linked to Home.
- Primary navigation: Services, Industries, Work, Resources, About, and Contact.
- Approved utility links and site search when enabled.
- Skip link and current-location behavior.

**Expected user action:** Select a known destination, open navigation, use search, or continue to the hero.

**Conversion goal:** Reduce time to relevant content for visitors who arrive with an established task.

**Requirements:** Follow the Information Architecture and Design System interaction, keyboard, focus, mobile, and sticky-header standards.

### 2. Hero Section

**Purpose:** Communicate what Tatros is, the class of problems it addresses, and the most appropriate initial actions within the first content view.

**Content:**

- One descriptive page heading identifying Tatros as a technology consulting and digital engineering company.
- A concise supporting statement connecting AI, software, cloud, web, mobile, IT support, and consulting to measurable organizational outcomes.
- Primary CTA to Contact or a consultation pathway.
- Secondary CTA to Services or Case Studies.
- Optional restrained visual that communicates systems, engineering, or transformation without obscuring content.

**Expected user action:** Initiate a qualified conversation or explore capabilities and evidence.

**Conversion goal:** Produce informed primary CTA engagement and immediate progression to a relevant evaluation path.

**Content constraints:**

- Do not use a rotating carousel, autoplay background video, animated headline, or multiple competing CTAs.
- Avoid vague claims such as “transforming the future” without explaining the service context.
- Do not place essential meaning inside imagery.

### 3. Trust Bar

**Purpose:** Provide rapid, verifiable reassurance without interrupting orientation.

**Content:** One concise class of approved trust evidence, such as verified client logos, current certifications, validated delivery metrics, or a factual statement about supported markets and capabilities.

**Expected user action:** Continue with greater confidence or follow a relevant credential link where additional validation is available.

**Conversion goal:** Reduce uncertainty before service evaluation.

**Publication gate:**

- Client logos require permission and a current relationship record.
- Certifications require verification, owner, scope, and expiry tracking.
- Metrics require a documented source, definition, time period, and review owner.
- If no strong evidence is approved, omit the section. Do not replace evidence with decorative or unverified claims.

### 4. Services Overview

**Purpose:** Help visitors map business needs to Tatros's core capabilities.

**Content:**

- Brief section introduction focused on integrated delivery.
- Seven service summaries: Artificial Intelligence, Website Development, Mobile App Development, Software Development, IT Support, Cloud Solutions, and IT Consulting.
- Each item includes a recognizable problem or outcome, a concise capability description, and a link to the canonical service page.
- Link to the Services landing page.

**Expected user action:** Open a relevant service detail or compare the full service portfolio.

**Conversion goal:** Increase qualified service exploration and establish breadth without creating an undifferentiated capability list.

**Content constraints:** Use consistent summary depth. Do not imply every engagement includes every service.

### 5. Why Choose Tatros

**Purpose:** Explain the operating characteristics that differentiate Tatros's approach and reduce perceived delivery risk.

**Content:** Three to five substantiated differentiators, such as business-first discovery, multidisciplinary delivery, secure and scalable engineering, transparent governance, measurable outcomes, and long-term support. Each differentiator should explain the practical client implication.

**Expected user action:** Review the delivery rationale and continue to process, evidence, About, or Contact.

**Conversion goal:** Shift evaluation from a service checklist to organizational fit and delivery confidence.

**Content constraints:** Avoid unsupported comparative claims, generic values without behavioral evidence, and claims of superiority that cannot be demonstrated.

### 6. AI and Innovation

**Purpose:** Establish artificial intelligence as a strategic capability while communicating responsible, outcome-led application rather than trend participation.

**Content:**

- A concise description of AI strategy, integration, automation, intelligent products, or data-enabled decision support.
- Explicit connection between AI opportunities, business objectives, data readiness, governance, security, and responsible use.
- One relevant example, case, or resource when approved.
- Link to Artificial Intelligence and optionally a related insight or case study.

**Expected user action:** Explore AI capabilities, evaluate readiness content, or review evidence.

**Conversion goal:** Generate qualified AI interest and distinguish responsible advisory and engineering from generic AI promotion.

**Content constraints:** Do not imply guaranteed outcomes, autonomous replacement of human judgment, proprietary capability that does not exist, or compliance that has not been verified.

### 7. Featured Solutions

**Purpose:** Translate individual services into integrated responses to high-priority organizational problems.

**Content:** Two to four curated solution themes based on validated demand. Candidate themes may include digital product delivery, legacy modernization, workflow automation, cloud enablement, data-informed operations, or managed technology support. Each theme should identify the problem, affected audience, combined capabilities, and appropriate destination.

**Expected user action:** Select a solution theme that resembles the visitor's challenge.

**Conversion goal:** Improve relevance for problem-aware users who do not begin with a technical service category.

**Governance:** A featured solution is a curated cross-service pathway, not necessarily a launch sitemap page. Create a dedicated URL only when unique search demand, content depth, ownership, and maintenance justify it.

### 8. Industries We Serve

**Purpose:** Demonstrate relevance to different operating and buying environments.

**Content:**

- Startups.
- Small Business.
- Enterprise.
- Government.
- For each, a concise statement of characteristic needs or constraints and a link to the canonical industry page.

**Expected user action:** Self-select an organizational context and view tailored capabilities, considerations, and evidence.

**Conversion goal:** Improve persona routing and support longer evaluation journeys.

**Content constraints:** Do not claim sector specialization or regulatory competence without current evidence. Use “audience” or “organizational context” internally if research shows “Industries” is not the most accurate label for these four categories.

### 9. Case Studies

**Purpose:** Substantiate Tatros's capabilities through real delivery context and outcomes.

**Content:** Two or three approved featured case studies representing meaningful capability or audience breadth. Each card should include client or anonymized context, challenge, Tatros role, relevant services, and measured or observable outcome. Include a link to all Case Studies.

**Expected user action:** Open a case-study detail or browse the evidence library.

**Conversion goal:** Reduce perceived delivery risk and provide shareable evidence for technical, executive, and procurement stakeholders.

**Publication gate:**

- Confirm client approval, confidentiality, accuracy, and outcome methodology.
- Clearly label anonymized or composite presentation; do not present hypothetical work as delivered client work.
- If no publishable cases are available, use verified portfolio evidence or omit the section until ready.

### 10. Technology Stack

**Purpose:** Provide technical evaluators with evidence of platform breadth and implementation competence without defining Tatros by vendor logos.

**Content:** Curated technology categories such as AI and data, application engineering, cloud and infrastructure, integration, quality, and observability. Display only technologies with verified current capability. Link to relevant services or technical evidence rather than presenting a noninteractive logo wall.

**Expected user action:** Confirm compatibility at a high level and continue to a relevant technical service, case, or contact pathway.

**Conversion goal:** Address technical-fit questions and support internal evaluator confidence.

**Publication gate:**

- Technology names and logos require capability-owner approval and trademark-compliant use.
- Vendor logos must not imply partnership or certification without documented authorization.
- The section should be omitted or reframed as engineering capabilities if a maintained stack inventory is unavailable.

### 11. Client Testimonials

**Purpose:** Provide qualitative evidence of collaboration quality, delivery behavior, and client outcomes.

**Content:** One to three concise, attributable testimonials with the speaker's approved name, role, organization, engagement context, and permission. Link to a related case study when available.

**Expected user action:** Validate partnership fit and continue to evidence, process, or Contact.

**Conversion goal:** Reduce interpersonal and delivery-risk concerns that technical descriptions cannot address alone.

**Publication gate:**

- Maintain written permission and approved wording.
- Do not invent, paraphrase without approval, or use unattributed testimonials.
- Do not use a carousel as the only way to access testimonials.
- If approved testimonials are unavailable, omit this section; do not substitute employee statements as client evidence.

### 12. Our Process

**Purpose:** Make the engagement model understandable and reduce uncertainty about what happens after contact.

**Content:** A concise lifecycle such as Understand, Define, Design, Build, Validate, Launch, and Improve, consolidated into four to six comprehensible phases. Explain activities, decisions, client participation, and outcomes at a summary level. Link to About, a future approach page if justified, or Contact.

**Expected user action:** Understand engagement expectations and advance toward a discussion.

**Conversion goal:** Reduce process ambiguity, demonstrate discipline, and improve inquiry readiness.

**Content constraints:** Present the lifecycle as an adaptable framework, not a promise that every engagement follows an identical sequence.

### 13. Latest Insights

**Purpose:** Demonstrate current expertise and provide a lower-commitment path for research-oriented visitors.

**Content:** Up to three current, durable articles or resources selected for audience relevance rather than publication recency alone. Each item includes type, title, short summary, publication or update date, and topic. Include a link to Resources or Blog.

**Expected user action:** Read an insight, explore the resource library, or return later.

**Conversion goal:** Support subject-matter credibility, organic discovery, return visits, and assisted conversions.

**Content constraints:** Do not label content “latest” unless publishing operations can keep the selection current. Use “Featured Insights” when manual curation is more sustainable.

### 14. Call to Action

**Purpose:** Provide a clear decision point after the visitor has received capability, audience, evidence, and process context.

**Content:**

- A concise invitation to discuss a business or technology challenge.
- Primary CTA to Contact.
- Optional secondary route to Services, Case Studies, or FAQ for visitors not ready to inquire.
- Brief expectation-setting statement describing what happens after selection.

**Expected user action:** Start a qualified inquiry or continue evaluation through the most relevant lower-commitment path.

**Conversion goal:** Convert informed interest into contact intent without pressure or artificial urgency.

**Content constraints:** Avoid guaranteed response times until operationally approved. Do not request data within the homepage CTA itself.

### 15. Footer

**Purpose:** Provide navigation recovery, corporate verification, legal access, and secondary pathways at the end of the page.

**Content:** Approved service, company, exploration, legal, contact, and social groups; legal identity; copyright information; and any verified governance links.

**Expected user action:** Locate a missed destination, verify Tatros, access legal information, or make contact.

**Conversion goal:** Recover unresolved journeys and support due diligence.

**Requirements:** Follow the approved footer architecture and design-system component contract. Do not reproduce the entire sitemap or publish unverified credentials.

### Section Dependency Summary

| Section | Minimum launch dependency | Fallback if dependency is unavailable |
|---|---|---|
| Trust Bar | Approved and verifiable trust evidence | Omit |
| AI and Innovation | Approved capability statement and destination | Integrate concise AI item into Services Overview |
| Featured Solutions | Validated problem themes and maintained destinations | Omit until demand is established |
| Case Studies | Publishable case evidence | Use approved Portfolio items or omit |
| Technology Stack | Maintained capability inventory and approved marks | Use text-based engineering capability categories or omit |
| Client Testimonials | Written approval and attribution | Omit |
| Latest Insights | Current publishing inventory and owner | Use Featured Insights or omit |

## User Journey

### Core Journey

```text
Arrival
→ Identity and value orientation
→ Capability or audience relevance
→ Evidence and delivery validation
→ Risk reduction and process understanding
→ Contact decision
→ Confirmation and next-step expectations
```

### Journey by Intent

| Visitor intent | Likely homepage path | Preferred next destination |
|---|---|---|
| Understand Tatros | Hero → Why Choose Tatros → Industries → Case Studies | About or Contact |
| Solve a known technical need | Hero → Services Overview → relevant Service → evidence | Contact |
| Solve an undefined business problem | Hero → Featured Solutions → Industry → Process | Contact or FAQ |
| Evaluate AI capability | Hero or Services → AI and Innovation → evidence or insight | Artificial Intelligence or Contact |
| Validate delivery capability | Trust Bar → Case Studies → Technology Stack → Process | Case Study, About, or Contact |
| Conduct enterprise due diligence | Industries: Enterprise → Case Studies → Process → Footer governance | About, policies, or Contact |
| Conduct government market research | Industries: Government → relevant Services → evidence → governance links | Government page or Contact |
| Research before engagement | Latest Insights → related Service or Case Study | Resource, Blog, or Contact |

### Journey Rules

- Each major section provides one primary onward path and no more than one necessary secondary path.
- Users must be able to bypass homepage sequencing through global navigation.
- Deep-link destinations must preserve orientation and provide their own onward paths; the homepage is not a mandatory funnel step.
- Contact links may preserve non-sensitive source context to improve routing.
- Return visitors should not be forced through introductory interactions, animation, or modal promotions.
- Journey success should be evaluated by task completion and inquiry quality, not by forcing every visitor to Contact.

## Conversion Strategy

### Primary CTA

**Intent:** Begin a qualified conversation about a business or technology need.

**Recommended destination:** `/contact/`.

**Label criteria:** Use a specific, low-ambiguity action such as “Discuss your project” or “Contact Tatros,” subject to content and usability validation. The label must accurately describe the next step and must not imply a free consultation, estimate, audit, or response time unless operationally guaranteed.

**Placement:**

- Global header.
- Hero.
- Contextually after high-intent evidence or process content where justified.
- Final CTA section.
- Footer contact route.

Repeated placement should provide recovery at natural decision points; it should not create visual pressure or compete with section-specific actions.

### Secondary CTA

**Intent:** Support visitors who need more evidence before contact.

The secondary action should be contextual:

- Hero: Explore Services or View Case Studies.
- Service content: View the relevant service.
- Industry content: Explore the relevant audience page.
- Evidence content: Read the case study.
- Final CTA: Explore Services, Case Studies, or FAQ.

Do not use multiple visually equivalent secondary CTAs in one region.

### Trust Signals

Prioritize verifiable evidence in this order:

1. Relevant case studies with context and outcomes.
2. Approved client attribution and testimonials.
3. Current certifications or formal partner status.
4. Transparent process, governance, accessibility, privacy, and security information.
5. Named expertise and current insights.
6. Validated operational or outcome metrics.

Every trust signal requires a source, approval status, owner, and review date. Decorative counters and generic claims do not qualify as trust evidence.

### Lead Generation

- Optimize for qualified opportunity creation rather than maximum submission volume.
- Route all homepage contact actions to one governed contact experience unless research proves a need for distinct forms.
- Keep initial fields limited to identity, contact method, organization where relevant, broad need, and a free-text description.
- Treat budget and timeline as optional or range-based unless qualification operations require them.
- Do not request confidential data, credentials, health information, government-sensitive content, or detailed proprietary requirements through the initial form.
- Measure form starts, field-level errors, abandonment, completion, lead quality, source section, and assisted paths subject to privacy requirements.

### Contact Strategy

- Explain what information Tatros needs and what happens after submission.
- Publish response-time expectations only when there is an accountable operational commitment.
- Provide a general alternative contact method for form failure and accessibility needs.
- Preserve entered information after validation errors.
- Provide an accessible confirmation state with next steps and privacy context.
- Establish routing rules for startup, commercial, enterprise, government, partnership, career, and support inquiries.
- Do not route existing-client support or job applications through a new-business form when dedicated channels exist.

### Conversion Measurement

| Stage | Example event | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Orientation | Hero CTA or primary navigation selection | Visitor identified an initial route |
| Relevance | Service, solution, or industry selection | Visitor found a plausible fit |
| Validation | Case, testimonial, process, stack, or About engagement | Visitor sought evidence or risk reduction |
| Intent | Contact selection | Visitor expressed contact intent |
| Completion | Valid contact submission | Inquiry completed |
| Quality | Qualified opportunity classification | Inquiry matched Tatros's engagement criteria |

Analytics must not treat scroll depth, time on page, or CTA click-through as standalone success. Evaluate these signals with downstream behavior and lead quality.

## Mobile Strategy

### Mobile Principles

- Preserve content priority and essential actions rather than merely stacking desktop sections.
- Use concise summaries with explicit links to deeper content.
- Maintain comfortable touch targets, readable line lengths, predictable focus, and sufficient spacing.
- Avoid horizontal carousels for essential services, industries, cases, testimonials, or insights.
- Defer nonessential media and motion without delaying primary content.
- Keep persistent UI compact; do not allow headers, banners, or sticky CTAs to dominate the viewport.

### Section Adaptation

| Section | Mobile behavior |
|---|---|
| Header | Use the approved disclosed mobile navigation. Preserve Services, Industries, Work, Resources, About, and Contact. Manage focus and background interaction. |
| Hero | Use a single content column. Keep the heading, supporting statement, and primary action visible early. Place the secondary action after the primary. Use media only when it does not delay content. |
| Trust Bar | Wrap concise text evidence into readable rows. Use a static grid or list for logos; avoid auto-scrolling marquees. |
| Services Overview | Present a vertical list or compact grid. Keep all seven services discoverable without a carousel. Avoid overly long card summaries. |
| Why Choose Tatros | Stack differentiators in logical order. Preserve the practical explanation, not only headings or icons. |
| AI and Innovation | Use one content column and an optimized static visual if needed. Keep governance and responsible-use context visible. |
| Featured Solutions | Use vertically ordered summaries. Place the problem and outcome before technical capability detail. |
| Industries We Serve | Use a list or two-column arrangement only when labels and summaries remain readable. Preserve all audience paths. |
| Case Studies | Present selected cases as a vertical list. Keep context and outcome visible; do not require hover. |
| Technology Stack | Group technology names by capability. Allow wrapping and progressive disclosure; do not create an unreadable logo cloud. |
| Client Testimonials | Show testimonials in document order. If disclosure is used, preserve attribution and do not hide every quote by default. |
| Our Process | Use a vertical ordered sequence with clear phase relationships. Do not depend on a horizontal timeline. |
| Latest Insights | Stack cards with consistent metadata and meaningful titles. Optimize images and avoid loading noncritical media before use. |
| Call to Action | Stack actions, keep the primary action first, and provide sufficient separation to prevent accidental selection. |
| Footer | Collapse groups only if headings remain operable, state is communicated, and all legal and contact links remain discoverable. |

### Mobile Validation

Test at compact widths, landscape orientation, split-screen conditions, 200% and 400% zoom equivalents, text enlargement, keyboard use, touch use, and intermediate widths. Do not infer touch capability from viewport size.

## Accessibility Considerations

The homepage must meet the project's WCAG 2.2 Level AA baseline and any stricter contractual or jurisdictional requirements.

### Structure and Semantics

- Provide one descriptive first-level heading and a logical heading hierarchy.
- Use appropriate landmarks for header, navigation, main content, and footer.
- Include a skip link as the first focusable control.
- Represent lists, process steps, testimonials, and grouped links semantically.
- Do not use heading elements solely to achieve visual styling.

### Navigation and Interaction

- Support complete keyboard operation with visible focus and logical focus order.
- Ensure disclosed navigation communicates expanded state and restores focus appropriately.
- Use links for navigation and buttons for actions or disclosure.
- Avoid hover-only content and inaccessible drag or swipe requirements.
- Ensure touch targets meet the design-system minimum and applicable accessibility criteria.

### Content and Media

- Provide meaningful text alternatives for informative images and empty alternatives for decorative images.
- Keep essential information in text, not inside logos, screenshots, animation, or video.
- Caption and transcribe media as required.
- Attribute testimonials in understandable text.
- Use plain language appropriate to the content without removing necessary technical precision.

### Visual Presentation

- Validate text, link, control, icon, focus, and boundary contrast in every state.
- Do not use color, position, shape, or motion as the only indicator of meaning.
- Support text resize, reflow, user styles, forced colors, and high-contrast modes.
- Maintain readable line length and sufficient text spacing.

### Motion and Status

- Honor reduced-motion preferences.
- Do not delay or conceal content through entrance animation.
- Avoid autoplay carousels, background video with essential information, scroll-jacking, and disruptive parallax.
- Announce form, loading, and asynchronous status changes appropriately without unexpected focus movement.

### Validation

Test the homepage through automated checks, keyboard review, screen-reader testing, zoom and reflow testing, contrast analysis, reduced-motion testing, and manual semantic review. Test with representative content, including long titles, missing media, validation errors, and translated or expanded text where applicable.

## Performance Considerations

### Performance Objectives

- Deliver meaningful hero content without waiting for nonessential media or third-party scripts.
- Protect Core Web Vitals and project-defined performance budgets across representative devices and network conditions.
- Prevent layout shifts caused by images, fonts, embedded content, trust evidence, or dynamically injected sections.
- Preserve functional access when optional enhancement scripts fail.

### Section-Level Requirements

- Render the hero's essential heading, message, and actions as primary content rather than image text.
- Avoid autoplay video in the hero. If video is later approved, provide a poster, user controls, reduced-data behavior, reduced-motion behavior, and a strict asset budget.
- Reserve intrinsic dimensions or aspect ratios for images and logos.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold imagery and noncritical embeds; do not lazy-load likely above-the-fold hero media when it is part of the largest contentful paint.
- Use responsive images with appropriate source dimensions and modern formats.
- Limit homepage case-study, testimonial, and insight counts instead of loading complete collections.
- Prefer static lists to script-heavy carousels, marquees, and animated counters.
- Load only required font families, weights, character sets, and icon assets.
- Avoid third-party testimonial, chat, scheduling, tracking, or video scripts unless their value, privacy, security, accessibility, and performance cost are approved.

### Performance Governance

- Define numeric budgets during technical planning for JavaScript, CSS, fonts, images, third-party requests, and page weight.
- Test field and lab performance at representative compact and expanded viewports.
- Monitor largest contentful paint, interaction responsiveness, cumulative layout shift, server response, and asset failures.
- Include the homepage in performance regression checks before release.
- Treat third-party additions as architecture changes requiring owner and impact review.

## SEO Considerations

### Search Intent

The homepage should target branded and broad organizational intent around Tatros as a technology consulting and digital engineering company. Individual service, industry, problem, and informational queries should be served primarily by their canonical detail pages rather than forcing the homepage to target every keyword.

### On-Page Requirements

- Use one unique, descriptive page title aligned with Tatros's approved positioning.
- Provide one descriptive first-level heading that complements rather than mechanically duplicates the title.
- Write a concise meta description appropriate to the page's role; it is a search-result summary, not a ranking claim.
- Use headings to represent content hierarchy, not keyword repetition.
- Include descriptive internal links to Services, Industries, Portfolio, Case Studies, Resources, Blog, About, and Contact where contextually relevant.
- Ensure visible text supports important service concepts without listing repetitive keyword variants.
- Keep primary positioning and service information available in the initial document response and not dependent on client-side interaction.

### Structured Data

Use only structured data types that accurately represent visible, verified content and remain supported by current search-engine guidance at implementation time. Candidate organization and website data may include approved legal name, URL, logo, contact information, and verified same-as profiles. Do not mark up testimonials, ratings, FAQs, services, or organization attributes that are not visible, current, eligible, and substantiated.

### Technical Requirements

- Canonical URL: `/` on the approved production origin.
- Include Home in the XML sitemap and permit indexing in production.
- Use stable internal URLs defined by the Information Architecture document.
- Maintain valid status codes, canonical host behavior, and redirect rules.
- Provide indexable text alternatives to essential visual or media content.
- Ensure mobile content parity and avoid hidden desktop-only evidence.
- Control tracking parameters and duplicate origin variants through canonicalization and redirects.
- Provide share metadata using approved title, description, and image assets.

### Content Quality

- Use specific, substantiated language and identify Tatros's role clearly.
- Avoid doorway content, hidden text, fabricated expertise, or location and service combinations unsupported by real operations.
- Keep featured cases, insights, certifications, and technology claims current.
- Assign an owner and review date to homepage content with operational or factual claims.

### SEO Measurement

Monitor branded and non-branded impressions, click-through, landing-page engagement, onward journeys, assisted conversions, indexation, rich-result eligibility where applicable, and search appearance changes. Interpret ranking and traffic alongside inquiry quality and target-audience relevance.

## Future Expansion

The homepage must be able to evolve without becoming an index of every new capability or campaign.

### Candidate Enhancements

- Additional industry or sector pathways after expertise and demand are validated.
- Dedicated responsible AI, security, accessibility, or governance evidence.
- Current certifications and formal partnerships with verification controls.
- Research reports, webinars, tools, or events within the Resources model.
- Regional or language-specific entry points following an approved internationalization strategy.
- A compact recruitment signal when sustained hiring warrants homepage prominence.
- Personalized content only where it provides measurable user value and has transparent privacy, consent, fallback, and testing models.
- Account or client-portal access only when a distinct authenticated product exists.

### Expansion Rules

- A new homepage section requires a validated user need, business objective, owner, content source, measurement plan, and retirement condition.
- Do not add a section solely because a new page or service exists; use existing service, industry, evidence, or resource collections first.
- Time-bound campaign content must define start date, end date, fallback, and responsible owner.
- New third-party embeds require performance, privacy, security, accessibility, and legal review.
- New claims and trust signals require substantiation and review cadence.
- Structural changes require cross-functional review across UX, content, design, frontend engineering, accessibility, analytics, SEO, and the accountable business owner.

### Review and Optimization

The homepage should be reviewed using:

- Persona-based usability testing.
- First-click and navigation testing.
- Accessibility evaluation.
- Search and behavioral analytics.
- Contact-path and lead-quality analysis.
- Content accuracy and evidence audits.
- Performance monitoring.
- Controlled experiments where traffic, risk, and measurement quality are sufficient.

Optimization should address a defined user or business problem. Experiments must preserve accessibility, performance, privacy, brand integrity, and truthful representation. A short-term click-through improvement is not sufficient if it reduces inquiry quality, comprehension, or trust.
